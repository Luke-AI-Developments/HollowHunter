# SHADOW HUNTER — Build-in-Public Content Plan
*The longest-lead marketing task. Goal: grow an audience NOW so launch hits real installs, not silence. Zero-cost, solo-friendly.*

---

## The story we're telling
People follow the **journey**, not just the game. Our narrative: *"A solo dev building his dream Solo-Leveling-style fitness RPG where your real workouts level up your hunter."* Underdog + cool-concept + a genuine passion project — inherently shareable. Sell the **concept in the first 2 seconds** of every post.

**Do NOT push the "built with AI" angle** — anti-AI sentiment in gaming/creative communities is strong and it invites "AI slop" dismissals before anyone judges the game. The tooling is nobody's business; you're the designer and driving force. Lead with vision, fitness, and the solo grind. (Note: the higher-scrutiny AI element at launch is *art*, not code — see below.)

## Tagline, mission & the two audiences (north star)
- **Tagline:** **"Build your character by building your body."** Use it everywhere — bio, captions, store listing.
- **Mission:** *Productive play — progress in the game IS progress in your life.* The rare game that's *good for you*. Lean into anti-escapism.
- **Alternate content between the two audiences** (same footage, two emotional angles, double the reach):
  - **A — "I'd rather game than train":** *"A game that levels up your real life."* Hooks: "I built a game to trick myself into exercising — and it's working." · "Your character only gets stronger if you do." · "Leveling up in-game = leveling up IRL."
  - **B — heavy fitness crowd (#gymtok):** *"The game that respects the grind you're already doing."* Hooks: "You already put in the work — now it builds something." · **"You can't buy strength here. You earn it. Just like the gym."** · "The strongest player is the one who actually trains."

## Platforms (record once, post everywhere)
- **TikTok** — primary. Best viral reach; "solo dev builds X" is a proven genre.
- **YouTube Shorts** — cross-post the same vertical clips; long-form devlogs later.
- **Instagram Reels** — cross-post.
- **X/Twitter** — dev community: #gamedev #indiedev #godot #screenshotsaturday.
- **Reddit** (carefully, follow each sub's rules) — r/godot, r/IndieDev, r/gamedev, plus fitness-gaming / anime-game niches.
- **Discord** — our own server, stood up once there's a trickle of interest.
Focus effort on **TikTok + Shorts + X**. One recording → all three.

## Content pillars (rotate these)
1. **Concept hook** — "your real steps level up your hunter." Re-explain regularly; new viewers each time.
2. **Progress clips** — screen-record the app on your phone after each patch ("today gear started dropping"). *You already generate this footage every session.*
3. **Behind-the-scenes** — the design process, the grind, the vision (the *what* and *why* — never the AI tooling).
4. **Fitness payoff** — "walked 10k steps IRL → leveled up in-game." Ties both audiences.
5. **Lore/design teasers** — a monster, the shadow-army idea, a mechanic.
6. **Engage** — "which class are you?", "name this monster" — comments feed the algorithm.

## First 10 posts (ready to shoot)
1. **The pitch (pin this):** "I'm building a game where your real-life workouts level up your character. Here's how it works." Show steps → EXP → level on your phone.
2. "I designed 60 original monsters for my fitness RPG — here are 3." (show the concept art/placeholders + names)
3. "My real Garmin steps just leveled up my hunter — on my own phone." (the Garmin→game moment)
4. "Solo Leveling, but the leveling is *your actual fitness*." (concept + vibe)
5. "I got the GPS working on my phone — you'll actually walk to hunt monsters in the real world." (the spike win, framed as gameplay)
6. "You can't buy your way stronger in my game — only exercise levels you up." (the ethos = a hook)
7. "I built a shadow army system — beat a boss, it joins your team." (the CLAIM fantasy)
8. "Here's the gear/loot system I just added." (armor sets dropping)
9. "What should this S-rank boss be called?" (engagement post, show a monster)
10. "3 weeks ago this was a design doc. Today it runs on my phone." (before/after, momentum)

## Cadence & workflow
- **3–5 short clips/week.** Consistency beats polish.
- **Batch-record** on one day; schedule the week.
- Rough placeholder footage is FINE — "it's early" is part of the story. Don't wait for art.
- **Footage capture is automated into the dev loop.** Claude Code saves screenshots + short screen recordings from every live device-verification pass into a local (gitignored) `devmedia/YYYY-MM-DD/` folder, with descriptive filenames and a `CAPTURE_LOG.md` index. Phone screen recordings are natively 9:16 — exactly the TikTok/Reels format. **This means the raw material for posts accumulates automatically every session; the only work left is picking a clip and writing a hook.** No excuse for an empty content pipeline.

## Rules that make short-form work
- **Hook in the first 2 seconds** — say the concept immediately, no intro.
- **Text captions on-screen** (most watch muted).
- **Vertical, 15–40s.**
- Use **trending audio** on TikTok/Reels.
- **Consistent handle + name** across platforms.
- **Link in bio → waitlist** (once the landing page exists).
- End with a soft CTA: "follow to watch me build it."

## Setup checklist (do this first)
- [ ] Grab handles on TikTok / YT / IG / X (username-squat now, even before posting). Note: "Shadow Hunter" is a generic/possibly-taken name — grab a variant (e.g. play/dev prefix), and remember the title itself may change (trademark check pending).
- [ ] Set a consistent profile pic (the placeholder icon works) + one-line bio with the hook.
- [ ] Record + post **Post #1 (the pitch)** — even today, rough.
- [ ] Add link-in-bio once the waitlist page is live.

## What to watch (don't obsess)
- Which **hook/angle** gets the most views/saves — do more of that.
- Comments = free market research (what do people ask for?).
- Follower + waitlist growth over weeks, not day-to-day.

## Strategic note: AI *art* is the real scrutiny point (not code)
AI-assisted *coding* is invisible and non-controversial — nobody sees it, nobody cares. **AI-generated *art* is different** — gaming communities actively detect and criticise it, and it's the thing most likely to draw backlash at launch. Options to manage it later: hand-touch/clean up AI art heavily so it reads as bespoke, commission key/hero art from a human artist, or develop a stylised look distinct enough to avoid the "obvious AI" tells. Decide this before the art pass — it affects both the art plan and how loudly we show art in marketing. For now: show *gameplay and concept*, go light on showing raw generated art.
